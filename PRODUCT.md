# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Mobile-first casual gamers playing idle/incremental games in short sessions. They hold their phone in **portrait** with one hand — thumb does everything. They're looking for a low-effort dopamine loop they can check in on throughout the day: tap, watch numbers go up, feel clever, put it down, come back.

## Product Purpose
More Ducks is the incremental sequel to [QLM](https://github.com/bearjcc/QLM) (Quack Language Model): same chat-shaped duck Q&A, scaled into a satirical idle game about building an absurdly confident duck-powered AI industry. It exists to make you laugh, give you a satisfying progression treadmill, and quietly needle the tech industry while you're having too much fun to notice. Success looks like: players who came for the silly ducks and stayed because the loop is genuinely addictive — and QLM fans who recognize the joke when an ending they didn't see coming lands.

## Intended player journey
1. "Ha, Duck Intelligence — silly." (looks like a chat app)
2. "Hey, this is cute." (first quacks, Answerdex fills in)
3. "Huh, it's pretty fun." (hold Auto, buy a duck, feed gate)
4. "Yes, flood Australia — **more ducks**." (regions, collection chase)
5. "Wait, am I the villain?" (rent, opex, idle supply)
6. "…Oh. The whole thing is an allegory." (mechanical satire lands without a lecture)

Endings are hidden until they happen: bankruptcy, singularity, global swarm, orbital ducks, or a competitor bird after ~80 hours.

## Brand Personality
**Cozy, Silly, Addictive.** Comfort-food energy with a compulsive loop — like feeding ducks on a lazy afternoon that stretches into evening. The satirical edge lands softly; the ducks are up to something but you can't be mad about it. Warm summer day on a pond, not corporate or edgy.

## Anti-references
- **AI slop**: No flat card grids, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no identical icon+heading+text cards. If it looks like a template, it's wrong.
- **Generic web apps**: This is a game, not a dashboard. No admin-panel energy. Buttons should feel like tapping a duck, not filling out a form.
- **Dark/gritty idle games**: No neon-on-black, no cyberpunk, no "productivity tool that happens to be a game." The vibe is a pond, not a server room. *Exception:* Acts 0–1 intentionally mimic a modern mobile LLM chat (system light/dark, message bubbles) — satirical disguise before the pond aesthetic emerges through era progression.
- **Overly minimalist**: The game should feel alive and rewarding. Sparse white space and muted everything kills the dopamine loop.

## Early-game presentation
Acts 0–1 read as a familiar chat app (QLM sequel): thread, composer, Send/Auto — light or dark per OS preference. Pond-green sky, duck-gold rewards, and outdoor warmth **emerge with era acts**, not on first open. The contrast with later pond identity is intentional satire.

## Design Principles
1. **Thumb-first, always.** Every interaction reachable with one thumb in portrait. Big tap targets, bottom-anchored dock and composer, no precision required.
2. **Numbers should feel good.** Every counter tick, purchase, and milestone should deliver a micro-hit of satisfaction through motion, sound, and visual feedback. The number going up IS the game.
3. **The pond tells the story.** The visual environment evolves with progression — not through text dumps, but through color shifts, atmosphere changes, and ambient details. The satire lives in what the player notices on their own.
4. **Reward the return.** Offline progress, welcome-back moments, and session openers should feel generous. The game should be happy to see you.
5. **Silly is a feature.** Duck puns, absurd flavor text, and playful animations aren't decoration — they're the brand. Take the craft seriously, never the ducks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Standard mobile game accessibility: good contrast ratios, existing reduced-motion support maintained, 44px minimum touch targets (already in place). No formal WCAG level target, but body text contrast should hit 4.5:1 and interactive elements should have clear focus states. Colorblind considerations for any color-coded game states (affordable/unaffordable).
